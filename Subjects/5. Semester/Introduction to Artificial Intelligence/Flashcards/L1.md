# L1 - Course Intro

## Flashcards

What is artificial intelligence?::There is no single agreed definition; broadly, machines that think or act intelligently (rationally), possibly mimicking human cognition.

What are the four perspectives on AI in the Russell & Norvig taxonomy?::Systems that think like humans, systems that think rationally, systems that act like humans, systems that act rationally.

What is the Turing Test?::A test where a machine is considered intelligent if its responses are indistinguishable from those of a human.

What are the cognitive tasks needed for systems that act like humans?::Natural language processing, knowledge representation, automated reasoning, machine learning, and common sense.

What are the two extra issues in the "Total Turing Test"?::Computer vision (perceiving objects) and actuation (manipulating objects).

What is a rational agent?::An agent that takes the optimal actions, based on its knowledge, to achieve its goals (Russell & Norvig).

What is the formal definition of an agent as a function?::$f: P^* \to A$, mapping percept histories to actions.

What is bounded rationality?::The design of the best agent given available computational resources (time, memory), since perfect rationality is often unachievable.

What is the difference between a reactive and a deliberative agent?::Reactive agents have no memory; deliberative agents have memory.

List the six environment properties.::Accessible/inaccessible, deterministic/non-deterministic (stochastic), episodic/sequential, static/dynamic, discrete/continuous.

What are the main topics in AI mentioned in the course?::Search, knowledge representation, planning, learning, natural language processing, expert systems, robotics.

What is search in AI?::Structuring possible answers/decisions/actions into an abstract space and searching it; fundamental AI technique.

What are declarative and procedural knowledge?::Declarative knowledge deals with facts ("what"); procedural knowledge deals with how to do things.

What is planning?::Given a set of goals, constructing a sequence of actions that achieves them.

What are the three types of learning problems?::Supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning.

What is supervised learning?::Given input/output pairs, learn to predict the output for a new input.

What is unsupervised learning?::Learning patterns in input data without supplied output values.

What is reinforcement learning?::Learning to interact with the world from the reinforcement (rewards) received.

What does the course cover?::Fuzzy logic and control, reinforcement learning, and testing AI-based algorithms and control.
